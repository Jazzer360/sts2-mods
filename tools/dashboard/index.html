Is the game running smoothly?

The mod's own time per frame over the last 5 minutes: a busy frame (95th percentile, blue) and the slowest (red). A frame has 6.1 ms for the game and the mod together at 165 fps, 16.7 ms at 60 fps and 33.3 ms at 30 fps (the yellow, orange and red dashed lines, each shown once the frames come near it); the mod normally stays around 1–2 ms, and a frame over 30 ms is a stutter you can see. Stuttering is a pattern, not a frame: a slow frame on its own (a settings page building, a fight's first prediction, a scene loading) is listed as a one-off and colours nothing; slow frames that keep coming (3 or more, each within 10 s of the last) are a stuttering spell, amber, and two spells in 5 minutes or one lasting 30 s turn the panel red. Hitches: gaps over 50 ms between frames while the mod's share was small, the game's own; only those during a fight count, since the game loading a scene (the launch, menus, the map, a room, a fight's first seconds) always hitches. The launch's first 20 s don't count against the mod either (its code compiling on first use). The chart leaves out the launch until your first fight has settled (the Performance page's "skip the launch"). Below: the features that cost the most.

    Which cards cost the most to work out?

    For every card in your hand the mod runs one search per target: it plays the card on the board as it is, follows everything it sets off, then plays out the rest of your turn and the enemy turn to predict the tabs and the outcome. The turn forecast is one more search, without a card. A search's work is how many game states it copied: every random choice (a random target, a Lightning orb, a random card) splits it into one copy per outcome, so work is how much a card branches on that board. Rows are cards (all their targets together), the costliest first. The end of the turn is part of every card's search, so a board whose enemy turn branches makes every card cost more.

    What allocates while nothing changes?

    Every feature that allocated on a steady frame, however little. A frame is steady once nothing the mod reads has changed for a second: the board, the screen, the control under the mouse, anything held, the settings, searches running (the mouse moving over nothing doesn't count), with the game's window in front. The mod works things out when something changes and only draws them after that, so on a steady frame every feature should allocate nothing. Dev mode's own work is left out (the dashboard, and its once-a-second reuse checks, counted as dev-checks). Apart is the commonest gap between the frames it allocated on: 1 is every frame, 15 a poll every 15th frame. steady-watch is this check's own work. Each one is in the game's log too, the first time it's seen ([steady] lines).

    Trace

    Who allocates?

    Every allocation the trace sampled (the runtime names one about every 100 KB, with its type and the stack that made it), charged to whose code asked for it: the mod when any of its frames (SpireQoL, Sts2Mods.Core) is on the stack, even when the memory was allocated inside the game's, Godot's or the runtime's code it called; else the game (sts2, MegaCrit.*), else Godot (its C# bindings), else others (Harmony, other mods), else the runtime alone. Each sample stands for about 100 KB, so a small share is an estimate.

    Who uses the CPU?

    Full traces only: every thread's stack about every millisecond, a thread waiting (a wait, a sleep, a read) left out. Charged as allocations are (the mod when any of its frames is on the stack); the second bar is where the time was spent, the frame itself. A Memory trace has no CPU samples: set Record a performance trace to Full for them.

    Do the collections pause the game?

    Each garbage collection in the trace: its pause (dots, left scale; blue a quick gen 0, amber gen 1, red a full gen 2) and the heap after it (teal, right scale). On the launch's played time (decision #238: time with the game's window in the background takes no room), with the tracing levels shaded. A frame at 60 fps is 16.7 ms: a pause near it is a hitch you can see. A heap that keeps climbing between full collections is memory held on to.

    gen 0 pausegen 1 pausegen 2 pauseheap after (MB, right)

    What does tracing cost, across your sessions?

    The mod's frames at each tracing level, added up over every launch a trace here was read from (each trace's report carries its launch's frames by level, from the dev stream). Off against Memory and Full on your own play: the frame rate, the mod's time in its typical and its busy frame, and the whole game's new memory and collection pauses per second. Similar play makes the comparison fair; a launch with tracing on throughout has no Off row of its own.
