Is the game running smoothly?

The mod's own time per frame over the last 5 minutes: a busy frame (95th percentile, blue) and the slowest (red). A 60 fps frame has 16.7 ms for the game and the mod together (dashed lines at 16.7 and 33.3 ms); the mod normally stays around 1–2 ms, and a frame over 30 ms is a stutter you can see. The budget: up to 2 stutters in 5 minutes stays green (amber number), more turns the panel red. Hitches: gaps over 50 ms between frames while the mod's share was small, the game's own; only those during a fight count, since the game loading a scene (the launch, menus, the map, a room, a fight's first seconds) always hitches. The launch's first 20 s don't count against the mod either (its code compiling on first use). Below: the features that cost the most.

    What will the enemy turn do to you?

    The turn forecast for the board right now, as if you ended your turn: the HP you'd lose (a range when random targets decide it), the chance you die, and how many distinct outcomes it found (branches). "Complete" means every outcome was worked out with everything modelled; "capped" means the search stopped at a cap and Incoming Damage shows ?; "gaps" means something in the fight isn't modelled yet, and the reasons are listed.

    How likely is each amount of damage?

    Each bar is an amount of HP you could lose this enemy turn, its height the share of the random rolls that lead to it (all the bars add up to 100%). One tall bar means the outcome is certain; a spread means random targets decide it. Red bars include outcomes where you die.

    Which cards cost the most to work out?

    For every card in your hand the mod runs one search per target: it plays the card on the board as it is, follows everything it sets off, then plays out the rest of your turn and the enemy turn to predict the tabs and the outcome. The turn forecast is one more search, without a card. A search's work is how many game states it copied: every random choice (a random target, a Lightning orb, a random card) splits it into one copy per outcome, so work is how much a card branches on that board. Rows are cards (all their targets together), the costliest first. The end of the turn is part of every card's search, so a board whose enemy turn branches makes every card cost more.

    Is the game itself stalling?

    The longest gap between two frames in each quarter second (16.7 ms is a smooth 60 fps). Over 50 ms (the dashed line) is a hitch you can see. When the mod's own time at that moment is small (the chart on the left), the hitch is the game's (loading, a room change), not the mod's.

    Could memory clean-ups cause hitches?

    Memory in use (teal) and new memory allocated per second (amber), for the whole game process, the mod included. Red dots are full garbage collections, which can pause the game for a moment. A steady line is normal; allocation that jumps whenever you hover cards points at the mod's calculations.

    Do calculations finish within a frame?

    When the board changes, a frame waits up to 8 ms for the new predictions. Blue: a wait that finished in time (how long it took). Red: a wait that ran out, leaving the rest to the background (the tabs come a frame or a few later). Amber: how long the board then took to settle, with every tab final. A few reds on busy boards are expected; many, or amber over 100 ms, are worth a look.
