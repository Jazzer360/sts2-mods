### 305. The Intent Forecast's projection searches on a worker — active (2026-11-25)
Revisits decision #304, which ran the projection on the game's thread under a copy cap of its own (20,000). The projection's start is read on the game's thread (the adapter reads the game's objects: `SimAdapter.ProjectionStart`, from Incoming Damage's own state of the board and every living enemy's forecast moves), and `SimEngine.Project` runs as a search on a worker (`SimJob.StartProjection`, decision #107): the frame waits for it as for any search (`SimWait`, 8 ms at most), it waits for a background turn past `SimJob.FirstBudget` copies, and it has the shared caps of every search (the memory cap, `SimWork.MaxCopies`), no copy cap of its own. While it's on its way the forecast draws each move as the game reads it now (exactly as an unknown projection), and once it's over its key takes the projected hits and the rows are built again; a read replaced before its search ends cancels it. The board-probe reuse check compares what two reads say (their moves, and their projected hits only when both searches are over: `IntentForecastFeature.ForecastRead.Same`), not when their searches ended. The forecast audit starts its own projection as your turn ends (pinned), lets it search through the enemy turn, gives it its own wait as your next turn starts and counts a skip with its reason when it isn't over by then or failed. `SimPerformanceTests` measures a big board's projection (four enemies, three forecast turns, two random picks, three Lightning orbs, Juggernaut: 24,225 copies, 41,427 stepped, 68 steps, 11,628 states held, 161.5 MB).
*Why:* decision #107: card simulations and the turn forecast search on worker threads, the frame waiting 8 ms at most; a hover or a board change in a big fight could hitch the frame by the projection's whole search, and the reuse check ran it again.
*Replaced:* decision #304's synchronous projection with its copy cap of 20,000.
