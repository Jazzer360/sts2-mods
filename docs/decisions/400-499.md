### 400. A power is read from the hooks of the abstract game class it's built on, and a test the code makes once is asked once — active (2026-11-26)
A power or relic class is read from the hooks it runs (`HookReader.RunHooks`): its own, and those of the abstract game classes it's built on that it doesn't override. Before, only the hooks a class declares were read, so the temporary Strength, Dexterity and Focus powers (Piercing Wail, Dark Shackles, Flex Potion, Setup Strike, Helical Dart and the rest, 21 classes on `TemporaryStrengthPower`, `TemporaryDexterityPower` and `TemporaryFocusPower`) read as having no hooks at all: no model, no turn-end gap, nothing marked unknown. Read now: a get-only property of the base class (`Sign`, `IsPositive`, the class's own override where it has one), and the amount of a power its own hook takes off (`PowerCmd.Remove(this)`, then `-Sign * base.Amount`: the game's instance keeps its Amount, `SimState.RemovedByItsHook`). A temporary Strength or Dexterity power's own landing and amount changes (`BeforeApplied`, `AfterPowerAmountChanged`) never run in a simulation, which lands it as the Strength or Dexterity it gives (`SimAdapter.PowerTo`, the reader's `ApplyPowerStep`), so plays land them as before; what's new is that one already on the board gives its stat back as its owner's turn ends. The temporary Focus powers still have a gap (their Focus is applied by the applier passed in), so they stay unmodelled, as before.
An enemy move's step on a test the code makes once, before an earlier step of the same move changes what it tests, is asked as the move starts (`WhenAction.AskedAtMoveStart`): Entomancer's Pheromone Spit fetches its Personal Hive into a local (`Powers.OfType<PersonalHivePower>().FirstOrDefault()`, now read by the move conditions: present, and its Amount), adds one to it below 3 and gives 1 Strength, else gives 2. Asked as each step runs, the hive at 2 would read 3 after its first step and give both.
*Why:* a dev review of the Regent run: four forecast projections were short. An enemy hit by Piercing Wail was projected to keep the -6 Strength into its next attack (Knowledge Demon 4 a hit where the game said 9, Infested Prism 0 for 5, Myte 1 for 6), and the Entomancer's Bees after Pheromone Spit hit for 1 less (its Strength step was a reviewed gap the projection left out).
*Replaced:* reading only a class's declared hooks; the Entomancer's Pheromone Spit steps as reviewed gaps ("a move with no attack").

### 401. An intent's power chips sit above the number on an intent that shows one — active (2026-11-26)
The buff and debuff chips Intent Details and Intent Forecast put on an intent (decision #398's bottom-left corner of its `%Value` box) move to just above that intent's %Value box, at its left, when the intent shows a number of its own: the game's `%Value` text (an attack's damage, a status intent's count) or Intent Details' block number. An intent with no number keeps them bottom left. (Placed at the holder's top-left first, which is the number's own corner: the Doormaker's Strength chip covered its 30. Where the game lays out an intent with a number is logged once a launch in dev mode, [intent-corner], to check the place against.)
*Why:* the owner noticed the Toadpole's Spike Spit: its Thorns taken away goes on its only intent, an attack, and the chip covered the attack's damage. The owner's call: a chip never goes over the number of the intent it's on.
*Replaced:* the bottom-left corner on every intent (decision #398).

### 402. An attack on several targets deals them all before its hooks and its kills — active (2026-11-26)
One damage with several targets (an attack on every enemy: Hyperbeam, Dagger Spray, each of its hits) runs as the game's `CreatureCmd.Damage` does with a list of targets: each target's damage worked out and dealt in turn, then the results' hooks in the same order (`AfterBlockBroken`, `AfterDamageGiven`, `AfterDamageReceived`) and what they set off, then the killed die together (`CreatureCmd.Kill` after the results loop), so a death's `AfterDeath` comes after every target's hit (`SimEngine.HitAllRound`, `Damage`'s `deferred`, `TellDeferred`). Between the results and the kills the fight isn't ending yet (`SimState.KillsNotYetRun`), so what those hooks set off runs, as in the game. And a target the hit killed isn't told `AfterDamageReceived`, on every damage (the results loop: `!WasTargetKilled || !IsDead`, the same on both branches): a kill no longer sets off its target's own reactions (Personal Hive's Dazed, Skittish's note, The Gambit on a hit that kills it anyway).
*Why:* a dev review of the owner's Defect win: Hyperbeam killed The Lost and hit The Forgotten for 33 where the simulator said 35. Hit one target at a time, The Lost's death gave the Strength it stole back before The Forgotten's hit was worked out; the game works out every target's damage before any death.
*Replaced:* each target of an attack on every enemy hit, told and killed before the next (decision #301's `HitOnce` per target), and `AfterDamageReceived` told to a target its hit killed (the hand-written models' reading, written when the game told it). The parity recordings (Explosive, The Gambit, Personal Hive, Sic 'Em, Compost, the bonus tabs) take the scenarios this changes; the rest of each stays the hand-written models' (`ParityRecording`: only the scenarios that changed are written).

### 403. A card made in the fight is profiled as the board's monsters make it — active (2026-11-26)
A card the board makes into your hand (a model's `CardsMadeIntoHand`: Withering Presence's Wither) is profiled from a copy of the game's canonical card that each monster on the board has run its `AfterCardGeneratedForCombat` on, when that hook's code changes nothing but the card (`SimAdapter.GenerationTouchesOnlyTheCard`: no game command, no store of its own, read from its IL with the monster's own methods it calls): Aeonglass's `MatchWitherToUpgradeCount` gives a Wither its `FakeUpgrade` for each upgrade it has given (3 more damage each). A hook that does anything else isn't run, and the canonical card stands, as before.
*Why:* a dev review of the owner's Defect win: the Play Preview showed no HP lost from the Wither a Claw's play made, where the Wither hit for its upgraded damage at the turn's end.
*Replaced:* the canonical card's profile for every made card.

### 404. An enemy killed during its own move keeps its powers to the move's end on beta — active (2026-11-26)
On beta, `CreatureCmd.Kill` leaves a creature's powers on it while its move runs (`IsPerformingMove`), and `MonsterModel.PerformMove` takes them off once the move ends; main's `Kill` takes them off as it dies. The simulator follows the running game's code (a branch rule, decision #169: `BranchRules.PowersStayThroughTheDyingMove`, told by whether `PerformMove` calls `RemoveAllPowersAfterDeath`; main's way is `MainRule.PowersGoAtDeath`): while an enemy's move runs (`SimState.PerformingMove`), its powers stay listening after your Thorns kills it, so the hit of its that lands after the kill is told to them. A stun from them still stuns nothing dead (`Creature.StunInternal`).
*Why:* a dev review of the owner's Fool run: Thorns killed a 2-HP Bowlbug Rock before its 16 landed in your block, and the forecast audit reported its Imbalanced set off where the simulator said it wasn't. The game told the dead Bowlbug's Imbalanced of the fully blocked hit; the simulator had dropped its powers at its death. One generated single-player fight of the 600 records the same change (an Imbalanced enemy killed by your Thorns during its attack).
*Replaced:* a dead enemy's powers going at its death, mid-move too (decision #195's listener rule, round three: alive, or the one whose kill is being told).

### 405. A frame on an unchanged board doesn't read it again while its searches run — active (2026-11-26)
The turn forecast and the card tabs already reused last frame's work on a board the probe saw unchanged once every search was over (decisions #114, #130). They now do it while searches are still running too: the forecast hands back last frame's start (the board read and its search) while the probe is the same and that search runs (`TurnEndModel._pendingStart`), and the card tabs keep last frame's preparations (each card's play read and its searches) while the frame key is the same (`CardPredictions._prepared`), stamping each prediction for the frame as before. A search that missed its frame no longer makes every later frame read the board and every card again until it's done.
*Why:* a dev review of the owner's Fool run: with Simpleton on you and 67 cards in your draw pile, every board read builds the play of each card Simpleton could pick (9 ms with 40 different cards, 1 ms without Simpleton), and the searches took up to 128 ms. Frame after frame cost 31–57 ms (the card tabs about 20, the forecast about 12) on a board that had stopped changing.
*Replaced:* reading the board and every card on each frame until its searches were done.

### 406. How many cards a pick takes from your hand, when you choose, is your later decision: unknown — active (2026-11-26)
A take from your hand (or draw pile) by a pick whose count you choose (a selector whose least and most differ: Ashwater's 0 to any, Purity's up to 3, Gambler's Brew's discard; `CardEffects.CardsTaken.ChoosesHowMany`) is unknown wherever it could take a card (`TakeFromHandAction.ChoosesHowMany`, `SimEngine.ChosenCount`): the simulation carries why, so the preview, Incoming Damage and the tags show `?`, and the audits skip it. With no card to take it takes none, certainly. Before, such a pick took the most it could, as a fixed-size pick does: a certainty the game doesn't keep. It's the rule Neow's Fury's take into your hand already had (decision #167: a choice inside the play is your later decision).
*Why:* a dev review of the owner's Ironclad run: Ashwater was predicted to exhaust the whole hand (27 block from Feel No Pain, no cards left); the owner exhausted fewer, twice. The owner chose `?` over counting none (Guards' transform's way), and may later prefer counting none: then `ChosenCount` takes none instead, and the audits skip a pick that took more.
*Replaced:* a pick whose count you choose taking its most.
